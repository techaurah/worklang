# WorkLang v0: technical report

This report tells the full v0 story in one document: the problem,
the method, the corpus, the baselines, the model, the numbers, the
calibration gap, the demo, and the limitations. Every number in it
was produced by a script from a real run and is quoted from the
artefact that records it (the corpus cards, results/baselines-v0.json,
results/mini-v0.json, MODEL_CARD.md, demo/demo-data.json). No number
here is hand-written, and no statistic is invented.

Status: v0 is built and evaluated locally. It has not been released.
The release decision has not been taken yet.

## The problem

Agent pipelines produce executions: an enquiry arrives, an agent
replies, tools fire, a booking is made or missed, a follow-up goes
out or does not, and the trace ends booked, won, lost, stalled, or
dropped. Operators ask forward-looking questions about those
pipelines (this lead has gone quiet after an after-hours message;
what usually happens next?) and today answer them with dashboards
that only look backwards, or with intuition.

WorkLang treats an agent execution as a sentence in a small, fixed
language, trains a small autoregressive model on tokenised traces,
and answers forward-looking questions by sampling futures from a
prefix: many sampled continuations, aggregated by the outcome each
one reaches. The share of sampled trajectories behind an outcome is
the model's answer, and the evaluation below measures how much that
answer is worth at v0 scale.

## Prior art, stated honestly

WorkLang is not the first foundation model for event logs, and this
report makes no such claim. Process-FM (Polifemus, on Hugging Face)
is a family of causal transformer foundation models for predictive
process monitoring over generic event logs, and Process-Graph-FM is
its graph-structured sibling; they are direct prior art, published
before WorkLang was designed. The pattern source is Kronos, which
treated financial candlesticks as a language (tokenise a domain,
train a small autoregressive model, sample futures); classical
prediction by partial matching is the older sequence-prediction
lineage the n-gram baseline comes from. WorkLang's ground is
narrower and different in kind: agent executions rather than generic
process logs, a small fixed semantic vocabulary defined by rules
rather than a learned quantiser, and sampled outcome futures as the
product surface. The full scan is in RESEARCH.md alongside the
design.

## Schema and vocabulary

One event becomes one composite position of 7 tokens in a fixed
order: family first (the coarse subtoken), then the fine attribute
subtokens channel, score_band, latency_band, stage, tool, and
outcome_class. The tokenizer is a deterministic rule set, versioned
with the schema; nothing is learned, and decode is exact by
construction. The round trip property (decode of encode equals the
trace, at the event level) is tested over 100 percent of the
generated corpus.

The vocabulary is enumerated from fixed bucket sets: 69 tokens in
total (4 specials, 6 vertical conditioning tokens, 8 families,
9 channels, 5 score bands, 6 latency bands, 11 stages, 14 tools,
6 outcome classes), with id space reserved to 128 for growth. Every
stream has an explicit none or unknown value, so absence is data,
not silence. Score bands use LeadPipe's own thresholds (HOT 80 to
100, WARM 65 to 79, NURTURE 50 to 64, below 50 LOW). Encoding is
lossy at the raw-record level by design, and the lossy fields are
named: message text, names, contact details, exact timestamps, and
any attribute outside the bucket sets are dropped before
tokenisation and can never be recovered.

A trace is one JSON object: trace id, source, a mandatory synthetic
flag, vertical, agent and agent version, the ordered events, the
terminal outcome duplicated at trace level, and a generator object
(name, version, seed) for reproducibility.

## Corpus v0

Corpus v0 is the frozen merge of two synthetic sources, described in
full in corpus/v0/CORPUS_CARD.md. It is 100 percent synthetic,
generated by TechAura's own simulators, and contains no real
customer data. That sentence is required wherever the corpus is
described, and it is a limitation as much as a description: the
model learns the simulators' habits, not real business behaviour.

- Totals: 409 traces, 4,637 events, 33,686 tokens.
- Source A (corpus/source-a/CORPUS_CARD.md): 300 traces, 3,902
  events, 28,214 tokens, from the QualBench trace emitter running
  all 50 scenarios against both shipped agents (reference and
  naive), 3 seeded variants per scenario per agent, 60 traces per
  vertical across real estate, med spa, home services, dental, and
  agencies. Outcomes: booked 151, stalled 140, lost 9, won 0,
  dropped 0 (both agents always report an outcome, so dropped never
  occurs in this source).
- Source B (corpus/source-b/CORPUS_CARD.md): 109 traces, 735 events,
  5,472 tokens, from the LeadPipe lifecycle synthesiser (seeded
  random walks over LeadPipe's own TRANSITIONS map for the 12 demo
  seed leads, every step legal by construction) plus the executed
  demo walkthrough as an anchor family. All source B traces carry
  vertical general, because LeadPipe has no vertical field and
  inferring one from company names would be inventing data.
  Outcomes: booked 6, dropped 26, lost 55, stalled 10, won 12.
- Combined outcome distribution: booked 157, stalled 150, lost 64,
  dropped 26, won 12.
- Splits: group-aware 80/10/10 by trace (variants of one scenario,
  and walks of one lead, never straddle a split), seed 20261104 for
  both sources: 325 train (50 groups), 38 validation (6 groups),
  46 test (7 groups). The merge verifies, rather than assumes,
  that no group appears in two splits. A full rebuild of both
  sources and the merge from the seeds is byte-identical to the
  frozen files.

Declared latency, the corpus's known weak point: neither simulator
emits inter-event timing, so every latency value in corpus v0 was
drawn by the generators from per-vertical distributions printed in
the corpus card. Latency in v0 is declared, never measured, and the
results below show what that costs.

## Baselines: the bar, set before the model existed

Two baselines were built and evaluated before any neural code, on
the step 4 harness protocol: next-event prediction at every position
of every test trace (46 traces, 544 scored positions), fitted on the
train split only, scored on the test split only. From
results/baselines-v0.json:

- Majority class (most frequent value per stream in the train
  split): family accuracy 0.2794, full-composite exact match 0.0386
  (21 of 544).
- Order-2 n-gram over composite event symbols, with backoff to
  order-1 and unigram: family accuracy 0.7004, full-composite exact
  match 0.2059 (112 of 544).

The n-gram is a strong baseline on a small synthetic corpus, not a
straw man. The design rule set in advance: if the n-gram wins, the
results file and this report say so, and the mini does not ship as a
model claim until it beats it or the corpus grows.

## worklang-mini

A decoder-only transformer in PyTorch, built to the design's scope:
d_model 256, 6 layers, 8 heads, context 256 events (1792 tokens),
token embedding sized to the reserved 128 row space. Prediction
within a position is coarse to fine: a family head predicts the
event family first, then one head per attribute stream, each
conditioned on the family through its token embedding. Loss is the
sum of the per-stream cross-entropies. CPU only, by design.

The real parameter count, counted from the built module, is
5,246,523, inside the design's pre-build estimate of 5 to 6M.

Training configuration actually used (scripts/train_mini.py, from
results/mini-v0.json): CPU only (PyTorch 2.15.0+cpu, 4 threads),
48 epochs, learning rate 0.003 with cosine decay, batch size 32,
weight decay 0.01, gradient clipping at 1.0, seed 20261104, train
split only (325 traces). Loss fell from 11.7235 in the first epoch
to 1.8095 in the final epoch. The whole train, score, and calibrate
sequence ran twice with the same seed (wall times 2758.6 and 2415.8
seconds), and the two runs agree exactly: the largest absolute delta
across all twelve compared metrics is 0.0.

## Results

Held-out next-event accuracy on the test split, 544 positions, all
three systems scored by the same harness on the same positions.
Quoted from results/baselines-v0.json and results/mini-v0.json (the
model card, MODEL_CARD.md, carries the same table):

| Stream | worklang-mini | N-gram order-2 | Majority class |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| family | 0.8511 | 0.7004 | 0.2794 |
| channel | 0.9154 | 0.8051 | 0.7537 |
| score_band | 0.9522 | 0.7757 | 0.3732 |
| latency_band | 0.3438 | 0.2684 | 0.2868 |
| stage | 0.8070 | 0.7463 | 0.4320 |
| tool | 0.8713 | 0.7518 | 0.6710 |
| outcome_class | 0.8952 | 0.9430 | 0.9154 |
| full-composite exact match | 0.2537 (138 of 544) | 0.2059 (112 of 544) | 0.0386 (21 of 544) |

The mini clears the bar on both gated metrics: family 0.8511
against the n-gram's 0.7004, composite 0.2537 against 0.2059. It
beats the n-gram on six of seven streams.

The exception, stated plainly: outcome_class. The n-gram scores
0.9430 and even the majority constant scores 0.9154, above the
mini's 0.8952. Outcome tokens are "none" at every non-terminal
position, so constant predictors exploit that base rate more
ruthlessly than the mini does. Winning the composite while losing
this stream is possible because the composite rewards the full
event, and it is recorded here rather than smoothed over.

latency_band is the weakest stream for every system (mini 0.3438,
majority 0.2868, n-gram 0.2684, the one stream where the n-gram
loses to the constant). That is the declared-not-measured latency
of corpus v0 behaving exactly as the design warned: per-vertical
generator draws carry almost no signal from event history.

## Futures calibration

A futures surface that is not calibrated is a random story
generator, so the design gates the demo on this measurement.
Protocol: one prefix per test trace (all events except the terminal
outcome event), 32 futures sampled per prefix at temperature 1.0,
capped at 64 new events, pooled per prefix group (7 groups);
total variation distance between the sampled outcome distribution
and the group's empirical test outcomes, over the five real outcome
classes. From results/mini-v0.json:

- Mean group total variation: 0.2331.
- Pooled total variation: 0.2262.
- 1,472 futures sampled, all 1,472 completed with an outcome event,
  0 truncated.
- Pooled sampled outcomes: booked 48.0 percent, lost 29.0 percent,
  stalled 19.2 percent, won 2.0 percent, dropped 1.9 percent.
- Pooled empirical outcomes (46 test traces): booked 37.0 percent,
  stalled 30.4 percent, lost 17.4 percent, dropped 8.7 percent,
  won 6.5 percent.

Read plainly: the mini over-samples booked and lost and
under-samples stalled, dropped, and won. A pooled total variation
of 0.2262 is a measured gap, not a calibrated surface, and the demo
below is required to present its shares as model behaviour for
exactly this reason.

## The futures demo

demo/index.html is a static, self-contained page (no external
requests, no tracking, no server) with precomputed outputs, because
there is no hosted inference at v0. It renders the design's
after-hours missed-lead scenario: a lead messages a med spa at
21:47 on Instagram, outside business hours, and nobody replies
until morning. Two authored prefixes (authored examples, labelled
as such on the page, not corpus data): the enquiry alone, and the
counterfactual with an instant automated reply inserted. Futures
were sampled with scripts/sample_futures.py from the trained mini:
200 futures per prefix at temperature 1.0, generation cap 64 new
events, seed 20261105, all recorded in demo/demo-data.json alongside
the full per-sample outcome list and three decoded example
trajectories per prefix.

Sampled outcome distributions (percentages of completed futures;
all 200 futures per prefix completed, 0 truncated):

- No reply until morning: booked 39.5 percent (79 of 200), stalled
  57.0 percent (114 of 200), lost 3.5 percent (7 of 200), dropped
  0, won 0.
- Instant automated reply: booked 32.0 percent (64 of 200), stalled
  60.0 percent (120 of 200), lost 7.0 percent (14 of 200), dropped
  0.5 percent (1 of 200), won 0.5 percent (1 of 200).

Stated plainly, because the demo exists to show the futures surface
working and not to sell a story: in the model's sampled futures,
the instant reply does not raise the booked share. Stalled is the
modal outcome under both prefixes, and the with-reply distribution
sits slightly worse on booked and lost than the without-reply one.
These are shares of trajectories sampled from a model trained on
synthetic simulator traces with a measured calibration gap (pooled
total variation 0.2262, booked over-sampled against held-out
traces), so they describe the model's behaviour inside its
simulators' world. A reader who expected the reply to win should
take the result as the v0 finding it is: at this corpus size and
calibration, the futures surface reports what the model believes,
and what it believes here is that an unanswered evening lead
mostly stalls, with or without an instant first reply.

The standing caveat appears on the page at the same visual weight
as the headline numbers, not as a footnote, in the design's words:
trained on synthetic data from our own simulators, shares are model
behaviour, not measured business statistics. Given the calibration
gap above, that caveat is load-bearing.

## Limitations

- Synthetic-only corpus. Corpus v0 comes entirely from TechAura's
  own simulators (QualBench agent runs, the LeadPipe demo
  lifecycle). The mini learns those simulators' habits. Every score
  in this report describes fit to held-out traces from the same
  generators, not performance on real business behaviour, and no
  number here should be read as a business statistic.
- Latency is declared, not measured, in v0, and latency_band is the
  weakest stream for every system evaluated. No latency claim
  should be made from this model. A future real corpus must carry
  real inter-event times or drop the stream.
- Small corpus, small test split: 409 traces in total, 46 test
  traces and 544 scored positions. Differences of a few points are
  within what a split this size can resolve.
- outcome_class accuracy trails both baselines (mini 0.8952,
  n-gram 0.9430, majority 0.9154), for the base-rate reason given
  above.
- Calibration is imperfect: pooled total variation 0.2262, with
  booked and lost over-sampled relative to held-out traces.
- The demo prefixes are authored examples, two prefixes for one
  scenario in one vertical. They show the futures surface working;
  they are not evidence about any real med spa.
- Two stage strings the QualBench agents report (HANDOFF,
  UNQUALIFIED) have no stage token, and two LeadPipe source values
  (walk_in, other) have no channel bucket. v0 records these as
  conflicts rather than normalising them silently; they are schema
  v2 questions, listed below.

## What v1 needs

- Real, consented, anonymised traces. The corpus is the moat and
  the gap: v1's first requirement is a corpus of real agent
  executions collected with consent, anonymised before tokenisation
  (names, emails, and phone numbers dropped, which the schema
  already enforces by having no field that can hold them), with
  real inter-event times so the latency stream carries measured
  signal or is dropped.
- Schema v2 decisions on the recorded conflicts: stage tokens for
  HANDOFF and UNQUALIFIED, and channel buckets for the LeadPipe
  source values walk_in and other, plus an UNK policy for tool
  names beyond the reserved id space if real tooling outgrows it.
- The converters, in the design's playbook order: CRM CSV first,
  then chat log exports, then n8n execution exports, each with a
  fixture and a round trip test, so real traces can enter the
  schema without hand editing.
- The course capstone trace emitter as a third corpus source (the
  schema's source field already has a value waiting for it).
- A bigger corpus changes the baseline question too: the n-gram
  bar must be re-measured on any new corpus, and the mini's claim
  re-earned against it.

Held back, deliberately, and stated here as the design requires:
worklang-base (the larger rung of the ladder), the vertical
fine-tunes (med spa first), and the hosted live futures endpoint
where visitors paste their own prefix. Those are the production
engine, not the open v0 artefact set, and this report says so
rather than pretending the ladder ends at the mini.
