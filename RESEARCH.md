# WorkLang: competitor and prior-art scan

Compiled 2026-11-04 for the WorkLang design phase (Flagship 2, Master
Playbook). Every fact below comes from a source actually read during this
scan; sources are named per entry. Where coverage conflicted (for example
star counts for the same repo across articles), the number is not quoted.

## The honest headline

No project found in this scan does what WorkLang is specced to do: a
foundation model over the executions of AI agents and small-business lead
pipelines, with a semantic event tokenizer, converters for agent-native
sources (n8n exports, chat logs, CRM CSVs), and a product surface that
returns sampled outcome distributions (futures) for a live pipeline.

But the neighbouring territory is occupied, and two claims are therefore
off limits from day one:

1. "First foundation model for event logs" is false. Process-FM and
   Process-Graph-FM (below) already pretrain causal transformers on event
   logs and release weights openly.
2. "First to treat a workflow as a language" is false in spirit. Kronos
   owns that framing for financial candlesticks, and predictive process
   monitoring has modelled process traces as sequences since at least
   2017.

WorkLang's defensible ground is narrower and real: agent executions (not
enterprise ERP logs), business outcome futures (not next-activity point
predictions), and a corpus with ground-truth outcomes generated by
runnable simulators we own.

## 1. Kronos (shiyu-coder/Kronos): the pattern source

What it is, per the project paper summary and coverage read for this scan
(paper: "Kronos: A Foundation Model for the Language of Financial
Markets", arXiv 2508.02739; reported as accepted at AAAI 2026): a family
of decoder-only transformers for financial candlestick (K-line) sequences,
trained on data from over 45 global exchanges, MIT licensed, with a
specialised tokenizer that quantises continuous OHLCV data into
hierarchical discrete tokens. The tokenizer (documented in the Kronos
demo's DeepWiki pages) uses Binary Spherical Quantization and splits each
bar into two subtokens: a coarse stream (s1) and a fine stream (s2), and
the model generates coarse first, then fine conditioned on coarse. The
model family, per the explainx.ai summary of the repo: mini 4.1M params
(paired with a 2k tokenizer, 2048 context), small 24.7M, base 102.3M,
large 499.2M, with the large tier not open sourced.

Borrow:
- The two-level token design (coarse event family, fine attribute
  subtokens) and coarse-then-fine generation order.
- The model ladder with a genuinely small, CPU-friendly mini (4.1M) as
  the public entry point, and one tier held back.
- Sampled generation as the forecasting surface: Kronos forecasts by
  sampling token trajectories and dequantising, which is exactly the
  shape of WorkLang's futures surface.

Do differently:
- Our events are already discrete (a booking happened or it did not), so
  the WorkLang tokenizer is deterministic and rule based, not a learned
  quantiser. No reconstruction loss, no codebook training, and decode is
  exact by construction.
- Kronos models a market nobody controls. WorkLang models pipelines the
  user owns and can change, so its output must be actionable (which
  futures dominate, and after which intervention they shift), not just
  predictive.

## 2. Process-FM and Process-Graph-FM (Polifemus, Hugging Face): the direct prior art

Read directly on their Hugging Face model cards on 2026-11-04.

Process-FM: causal transformer foundation models for predictive process
monitoring. Given the prefix of a case (activities, resources,
timestamps), they predict the next activity and the next resource. All
models share one global vocabulary (activities are global ids 4 to 776,
resources 2 to 2739, per the card) and one inference script. The flagship
checkpoint fm-v6 is 27.4M params (d_model 512, 8 layers), pretrained on
corpus v3 (18 real plus 40 synthetic logs) for roughly 0.43B positions by
the card's table. A scaling suite runs sc5 (6.32M) through sc80 (88.19M)
to sc300 (306.4M, metrics pending on the card). Objective: causal
next-event modelling over 256-event windows. Weights and vocabularies are
MIT; raw real logs are not redistributed (they stay under their original
licences, mostly 4TU terms; BPIC 2018 is CC0); the synthetic half of the
corpus is generated by a directly-follows-graph mixer and released
separately as Polifemus/event-log-mixer under MIT.

Process-Graph-FM: the same author's graph variant (GPS style graph
transformer, 27.28M and 88.14M checkpoints), pretrained on a corpus of 18
real plus 300 synthetic logs, predicting next activity and next resource.
Its card is a model of honest reporting: at equal corpus and budget the
graph model does not beat the sequence model on zero-shot transfer, and
the card says so in plain words, with one-seed caveats stated.

Borrow:
- Proof that the recipe works at our scale: a 6.32M param causal
  transformer over event logs is a real, released artefact, so a CPU
  class WorkLang mini is a credible scope, not a hope.
- The synthetic-corpus pattern: generate synthetic logs with a mixer,
  release the synthetic half openly, keep real logs under their own
  licences. WorkLang goes one step further (our corpus is 100 percent
  synthetic in v0 and says so on every trace).
- Their reporting style: equal-budget comparisons, negative results
  printed, one-seed caveats. This matches the QualBench honesty standard
  and we adopt it wholesale.
- The global vocabulary idea, inverted: they map every activity and
  resource in every log into one large id space (thousands of tokens).
  WorkLang instead fixes a small semantic vocabulary (under 128 tokens,
  see DESIGN.md) so a trace from any business reads in the same alphabet.
  That only works because our domain is narrow; it is our main technical
  bet.

Do differently:
- Domain: BPIC-style enterprise process logs (loans, permits, IT tickets)
  versus agent-run lead pipelines for small businesses. Different events,
  different users, different buyers.
- Target: next activity and next resource (point predictions) versus
  sampled full futures aggregated into outcome distributions (books,
  stalls, drops) with confidence language.
- Inputs: their corpus assumes an existing XES-style event log. WorkLang
  ships converters (n8n exports, chat logs, CRM CSVs) because agent
  workflows rarely produce a clean log today.

## 3. Classical predictive process monitoring (PPM): the academic base

Tax, Verenich, La Rosa and Dumas, "Predictive Business Process
Monitoring with LSTM Neural Networks", CAiSE 2017 (read in full PDF):
LSTMs trained on completed cases predict the next activity of a running
case and its timestamp, generate the full remaining suffix of a case, and
predict remaining cycle time, outperforming tailor-made baselines on four
real-life logs. Public code exists (the ProcessSequencePrediction repo).
A 2022 Springer chapter on learning simulation models from event logs
(read for this scan) maps the follow-on line: DeepGenerator extends the
LSTM approach with n-grams and embeddings to generate whole traces, and
generative approaches (including GAN-LSTM work) produce timestamped
activity sequences; DeepSimulator combines a discovered stochastic
process model with a deep model for timing.

Borrow:
- The evaluation grammar: next-event accuracy, suffix quality, and the
  discipline of prefix-based evaluation on held-out cases. WorkLang's
  eval (DESIGN.md) follows it, with the two honest baselines the field
  would expect: majority class and n-gram.
- The finding that next-event models can generate full continuations is
  the academic licence for our futures sampler; we are applying a known
  technique to a new domain, and the technical report must say that.

Do differently:
- PPM assumes one organisation's historical log, trains per process, and
  stops at prediction. WorkLang pretrains across many simulated
  businesses in one vertical-spanning corpus and ships the model, which
  is the foundation-model move Process-FM already made for generic logs
  and nobody has made for agent pipelines.

## 4. LLM-on-logs work: traces as text

A 2025 survey-style source read for this scan (the "Building Temporal AI"
textbook, process mining section) describes the 2024 to 2025 research
frontier: transformer monitors with rich event encodings leading standard
BPI benchmarks, and a parallel line that serialises traces into token
sequences and prompts or fine-tunes a general LLM for next-activity and
outcome prediction. The PMF benchmarking paper's reference list (read for
this scan) names an example: Lupin, an LLM approach for activity suffix
prediction in business process event logs (Pasquadibisceglie et al.,
2024).

Borrow: nothing architectural. Serialising traces into a general LLM's
text vocabulary is the expensive path; our small fixed vocabulary exists
precisely so a 5M param model can do the job on CPU.

Watch: if a frontier lab ships agent-trace modelling inside a general
agent platform, WorkLang's moat is the corpus, the converters, and the
outcome-futures surface, not the model. That is the same lesson as the
Master Playbook's cautionary shelf.

## 5. Transaction foundation models (banking): the adjacent proof

Per a Medium research roundup read for this scan (October 2026):
Revolut's PRAGMA is a family of transformer foundation models for banking
event sequences, pretrained with masked modelling, aimed at credit
scoring, fraud detection and lifetime value prediction, reported at 10M
to 1B params; Nubank reports training and deploying billion-parameter
models on transaction sequences across several predictive use cases;
Visa's TREASURE is described as a multipurpose transformer foundation
model for transaction data.

Borrow: the strategic pattern only. Event sequences from one business
domain, one pretrained model, many downstream decisions. Banking proves
buyers accept the pattern; it also proves the winners own proprietary
event data at scale, which is why WorkLang's corpus strategy (simulators
now, consented anonymised traces later) is the long game.

Do differently: they model money movement for risk decisions inside one
institution. WorkLang models work movement (enquiries, replies, bookings,
follow-ups) for pipeline decisions across many small businesses, openly.

## 6. Process mining platforms (Celonis, UiPath, pm4py): the incumbent layer

Per comparison sources read for this scan: Celonis reconstructs how
processes actually run from event logs pulled out of ERP, CRM and ITSM
systems, then layers KPI and conformance monitoring and recommended
actions on top; UiPath pairs process mining with its RPA platform;
pm4py is the open-source library researchers use for discovery and
conformance pipelines (per the textbook source above).

Borrow: their event log mental model. A trace is a case, an event is an
activity with a timestamp and a case id (the definition used in the PMF
benchmarking paper read for this scan). WorkLang's trace schema keeps
that skeleton so process-mining users can read it, and adds the
agent-specific attributes (channel, score band, tool, handoff) that ERP
logs do not have.

Do differently: the platforms are retrospective (what happened, where it
deviates) and enterprise priced. None of them, per anything read in this
scan, offers a small open model that samples the future of a pipeline.
That gap is WorkLang's product surface.

## 7. What appears not to exist (stated with scan limits)

Within this scan (web search plus the sources above, 2026-11-04), no
project was found that combines: agent execution traces as the corpus, a
semantic hierarchical event tokenizer, converters from agent tooling
(n8n, chat, CRM exports), and sampled outcome distributions as the user
facing output. Absence in one scan is not proof of absence in the world;
the technical report at launch should repeat this scan and soften the
claim to "we found no" wording.
